For many years I’ve played FPS games, more specifically COD, and never really took a serious look at my audio settings beyond the in-game toggle for Enhanced Headphone Mode. I’d argue that going the extra mile and updating a few more settings on your PC and in-game goes a long way.

## Who and what is this article for?

- PC gamers 
- Windows 11 users
- FPS players
- Call of Duty players

## Why care about audio settings?

I know for most average people playing shooters, they don’t care about configuring their audio settings. They just want to play the game, and I totally get that. That’s why it took me so long to play around with my settings. The primary reason to care about audio in FPS games is that it helps you identify where your opponents are coming from. Improving your ability to hear not only improves your skill at the game but also immerses you further.

If you just want the exact settings to copy and paste into your setup without reading the full breakdown, here is everything you need to configure:

- **What You Need:** Your headphones and your headset's free companion app (like Turtle Beach Swarm II, SteelSeries GG, or Logitech G HUB) for mic and equalizer tweaks.

### Windows 11 Settings:
- **3D Spatial Audio:** Press Win + I > System > Sound > select your headphones > set Spatial audio to Windows Sonic for Headphones (free) or Dolby Atmos.
- **Disable Enhancements:** Press Win + R, type `mmsys.cpl`, right-click your headphones > Properties > Enhancements, and check Disable all enhancements.
- **Mic & Sidetone:** Set manual mic sensitivity in Discord/game so your voice hits ~75%, and turn on Sidetone (Mic Monitoring) via your headset app or earcup dial.
- **Equalizer (EQ):** In your headset app's EQ tab, slightly lower the far-left sliders (sub-bass) and slightly boost the middle-right sliders (2 kHz to 4 kHz) for footsteps.

### Call of Duty In-Game Settings:
- **Audio Mix:** Headphones Bass Boost or Home Theater (Speaker Output set to Stereo).
- **Volume Sliders:** Master: 80% to 100% / Effects (SFX): 100% / Dialogue: 55% / Voice Chat: 60% to 70% / All Music & War Tracks: 0%.
- **Hit Markers & Tinnitus:** Hit Marker Sound Effects: Classic / Hit Marker Volume: 50% / Reduce Tinnitus Sound: On.
- **Optional Boosts:** Toggle on Enhanced Headphone Mode in-game (free universal mode or paid Embody custom ear scan), or use your headset's built-in footstep preset (like Turtle Beach's Superhuman Hearing button).

## Adjust Windows 11 Audio Settings

The first place to start is configuring how your base system sends audio to your headphones. In this case, your Windows 11 operating system.

### 1. Turn on 3D Spatial Sound

Standard stereo only plays sound to your left or right ear, while 3D spatial sound lets you tell if someone is above, below, or behind you.

**How to set it up:** Open Windows 11 Settings by pressing Win + I, go to System > Sound, select your output device, scroll down to Spatial audio, and choose Windows Sonic for Headphones from the drop-down menu <Citation to="microsoft">(Microsoft, n.d.)</Citation>. If you want to take it a step further, optional paid upgrades like Dolby Atmos for Headphones are also supported.

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Windows 11 System > Sound screen showing the output device properties with the Spatial audio dropdown highlighted on "Windows Sonic for Headphones"].*

### 2. Turn off Fake Audio Enhancements

Windows has default sound enhancements (like artificial bass boost or volume leveling) that distort game audio and make footsteps harder to pinpoint.

**How to set it up:** Open the classic Windows Sound Control Panel (`mmsys.cpl`), right-click your headphones, open Properties, and uncheck all artificial enhancements under the Enhancements tab so the game sends clean, unfiltered sound straight to your ears <Citation to="microsoft">(Microsoft, n.d.)</Citation>.

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Classic Sound Control Panel properties window showing the Enhancements tab with "Disable all enhancements" checked].*

### 3. Set up Your Mic and Sidetone

Sidetone (also called mic monitoring) plays a tiny bit of your own voice back into your headphones. This keeps you from accidentally shouting in your room because you cannot hear yourself through the ear cushions.

**How to set it up:** First, adjust your microphone volume manually in Discord or your game settings so your voice does not distort or cut out when talking to teammates. To turn on sidetone, you will usually need your headset's free companion app (the official software from your headset's brand, like Turtle Beach Swarm II, SteelSeries GG, or Logitech G HUB) or a physical dial on the earcup itself.

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Discord or headset companion app showing the input sensitivity slider and mic monitoring/sidetone dial].*

### 4. Balance the Bass and Treble

This tip is a little more advanced, and it uses something called an equalizer (or EQ). An EQ is just a simple volume mixer for different pitches of sound, letting you turn booming bass down or crisp treble up. You do not need to buy anything for this: if you downloaded your headset's companion app in the last step, it almost certainly has a built-in EQ tab (and if your headset does not have an app, free Windows tools like SteelSeries Sonar work with any headphones). Out of the box, most gaming headsets crank up the bass to make explosions sound like a movie theater, which unfortunately drowns out quiet footsteps.

**How to set it up:** Open the equalizer tab in your headset's companion app and lower the sliders on the far left (the lowest bass) slightly. Then, give a small lift to the sliders in the middle-right (around 2 kHz to 4 kHz) so footsteps, reloads, and gear rustling pop out clearly.

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Simple EQ graphic showing sub-bass lowered and a gentle lift in the 2 kHz to 4 kHz range].*

## How should you adjust your Call of Duty audio settings?

Once you’ve configured your base system audio settings, you’ll need to update the in-game settings.

### 1. Pick the Best Audio Mix

Settings like PC Speaker flatten out quiet sounds, while Home Theater keeps a wide volume range so quiet footstep thuds do not get buried under gunfire.

**How to set it up:** Go to Settings > Audio > Audio Mix and select Headphones Bass Boost or Home Theater <Citation to="activision">(Activision, n.d.)</Citation>.

**Example Settings:**
- **Audio Mix:** Headphones Bass Boost (or Home Theater)
- **Speaker Output:** Stereo (or Windows Default)

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Call of Duty Audio settings menu highlighting the Audio Mix dropdown set to "Headphones Bass Boost"].*

### 2. Turn the Music All the Way Down

Dramatic background music swells during intense moments, which is the exact second you need quiet to hear someone sneaking up on you.

**How to set it up:** Go to Settings > Audio > Volume and drop Music Volume completely to 0 <Citation to="activision">(Activision, n.d.)</Citation>.

**Example Settings:**
- **Master Volume:** 80% to 100%
- **Gameplay Music Volume:** 0%
- **Cinematic Music Volume:** 0%
- **War Tracks Volume:** 0%

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Call of Duty Volumes menu with Music Volume set to 0 and Master Volume highlighted].*

### 3. Balance Game Effects Against Voice Lines

In-game announcers love to shout updates right when an enemy is climbing a ladder next to you.

**How to set it up:** Keep Sound Effects (SFX) Volume at 100% and lower Dialogue Volume to around 50% or 60% <Citation to="activision">(Activision, n.d.)</Citation>.

**Example Settings:**
- **Effects Volume:** 100%
- **Dialogue Volume:** 55%
- **Voice Chat Volume:** 60% to 70% (adjust so teammates do not overpower game sound)

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Call of Duty Volumes slider menu showing Effects Volume at 100% and Dialogue Volume at 50%].*

### 4. Soften Hit Marker Sounds

The loud clicking sound of landing shots can easily mask other enemies moving nearby during a gunfight.

**How to set it up:** Go to Settings > Audio > Hit Marker Sound Effects and change hit markers to Classic or turn their volume down slightly <Citation to="activision">(Activision, n.d.)</Citation>.

**Example Settings:**
- **Hit Marker Sound Effects:** Classic
- **Hit Marker Volume:** 50%
- **Reduce Tinnitus Sound:** On (replaces the piercing flashbang and stun grenade ringing with a duller sound)

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Call of Duty Audio setting showing Hit Marker Sound Effects toggled to "Classic"].*

## What headset features and add-ons are worth knowing about?

Depending on your headset, you might have extra tools built right in that make positioning even easier.

### Personalized Ear Profiles (Embody Immerse Gamepack)

Call of Duty includes an optional partner tool under Enhanced Headphone Mode that uses your phone camera to scan your ear shape, creating a custom surround sound profile made just for you <Citation to="embody">(Embody, n.d.)</Citation>.

**How it works:** The basic universal mode is completely free to toggle on inside the game. If you want the custom ear scan tailored to your exact head and ear shape, you can unlock the personalized profile as a paid add-on.

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: In-game Enhanced Headphone Mode setup screen and QR code mobile scan prompt].*

### On-Headset Buttons and Apps (Turtle Beach Stealth 600)

Sometimes headsets come with their own audio enhancements that are also configurable. As an example, here are some things you can do with the Turtle Beach Stealth 600:

**How it works:** You can press the on-ear Superhuman Hearing button to boost quiet footstep sounds with a single click, or open the companion Swarm II app on your PC or phone to switch sound presets and tweak your equalizer <Citation to="turtle-beach">(Turtle Beach, n.d.)</Citation>.

> *Visual Screenshot: [Image: Turtle Beach Stealth 600 earcup controls pointing to the Superhuman Hearing button and Swarm II EQ presets].*

## Concluding Thoughts

Everyone hears slightly differently, so spending five minutes testing what feels natural to your ears is the best way to lock in your setup.
